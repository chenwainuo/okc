# P3-m256: router m=256 (and m=128) on the H100 — pw29m, 2026-10-26

**Outcome:** the bounded attempt passed its kill rule.
- **m=256:** rt64k16b6z runs **12.42 in-prepare vs r6 14.85**. r6 is the F1 baseline: the lower of r6 timed before the rw build (14.88) and after the variants (14.85). That is **−2.43 box-µs**; the bar was −1.0.
- **m=128:** rt64k32b4z runs 10.34 vs rw64k32s8z 11.49, rp64k32s4 13.54 and r6 16.00.
- All 11 official router checks pass (logs/pw29m.log).
- Harness table: verify16 router 13.12 (pw29h 16.16, torch 23.02); verify32 15.82 (pw29h 19.78, torch 29.41).

## 1. What the stamps showed (globaltimer, MODE 16, thread 0, µs from the earliest CTA start, median CTA)
The cp.async kernel (rwk) spent **3.7–11 µs issuing its cp.asyncs** before its first wgmma. Each thread issues 64–96 16-B copies, and the issue stalls on the full LSU queue. The same threads run the MMAs, so the first wgmma started only after "issued" (first ≈ issued + 0.1), and the tensor phase never overlapped the stream. This was rwk's dominant fixed term at every geometry.

The KR-512 rings (rw64k16r6, rw64k16r3x2: 24 µs and more) stall in the refills: last data arrives at 25 µs.

**Fix (rtk):** a warp-specialised TMA kernel.
- **Roles.** Warps 0–3 are the consumer warpgroup. Warp 4 lane 0 is the producer, issuing three 2D TMA boxes per 64-k stage (x {64, TOKS}, hi {64, 64}, lo {64, 64}) into an NBUF-slot ring with full/empty mbarriers.
- **Zero warp** (warp 5, MODE 4): it zeroes 1/G of the tile, arrives with `atom.add.release`, polls the call's G arrivals, and meets the consumers on a named barrier (bar 2, 160 threads) only before the reds.
- **Why a separate warp.** A first version put the release atomic on consumer thread 0. That atomic waits for the CTA's in-flight TMA traffic, which pushed the first wgmma from 2.2 to 5 µs and cost +2.5 µs (rt64k16b6z 18.16 vs rt64k16b6 16.58).

**Reading the table:**
- **Halving the reds.** The 16-way K split of rt64k16b6z (4 MB of reds vs 8 MB) cuts the red issue from 1.6 to 0.8 µs.
- **No memset node.** The zero handshake is fully hidden (staged → ready 0.16), and dropping the memset node saves 1.1–1.5 rbench-µs.
- **Remaining fixed terms on the H200:** start → first data 2.2 µs; the last-stage MMA tail 0.6; staging 0.16; the red burst 0.8 plus drain. The stream itself (first → last data, 5 µs at 256 KB per SM) is the part that should shrink on the H200's HBM.

## 3. Failed or closed in this attempt
- **x multicast over a cluster of the 4 n-tiles** (rt*m):
  - cudaOccupancyMaxActiveClusters is 30 at 1/SM (120 CTAs < 128), so the grids run in two waves, and even the first wave's stream is slower (last data 11.5 vs 7.1).
  - With self-zeroing it **deadlocks**: the zeroing wait spans clusters that are not co-resident.
  - rt_go now refuses MODE 4 + 32 (−31) and any self-zeroing grid larger than the co-resident slots (−30), so a bad bake entry declines instead of hanging.
- **K split 8** (rt64k8b6z, 64 CTAs): 512 KB per SM, stream-bound at 20 µs.
- **256 tokens per CTA** (rtv*): the n256 tensor tail (+1.15) and 8 MB of reds.
- **Self-zeroing on the consumer thread:** see §1.
- **prefetch.tensormap at entry (MODE 64):** neutral (rbench ±0.1; first data 2.18 vs 2.18). The 2.2 µs first-data term is DRAM queueing of the whole first wave's requests (24 MB outstanding), not the descriptor fetch.

## 4. Shipping
- **File:** proj_rsi/candidates/pw29m/submission.py. Its router section is pw29h's plus the `rtk` / `rt_go` / `rt_map` / `rt_occ` code in RWCU, `RTV`, the RSPALL entries and the F1 baseline in `_rsbake`.
- **Bake set (RSP):**
  - m=128: rp64k32s4, rw64k32s8z, rw64k32r3x2z, rt64k16b6z, rt64k32b3x2z, rt64k32b4z, rt64k32b3x2bz.
  - m=256: rw64k32r3x2z, rt64k16b6z, rt64k32b3x2z, against r6 with the F1 baseline.
- **Build:** the shipped build compiles only these instances (34 s on the box).
- **H200 expectation:** the judge kept r6 at m=256 (15.68) over rw64k32r3x2z (16.00). The rt path removes about 2.3 box-µs of fixed terms (cp.async issue, the red burst, and the memset node via the zero warp, which is known to transfer). Expect rt64k16b6z at roughly 13.5–14.5 judge in-prepare vs r6's 15.7, i.e. −1 to −2 judge-µs at m=256 (6.6 score-µs per µs).
- **Risks:**
  - Self-zeroing requires stream-ordered calls and a co-resident grid (now checked at launch).
  - The x tensor map is encoded per call; under graph capture it is baked like a pointer.
  - x must be 16-B aligned (TMA); `_rscall` clones a misaligned x as before.

## 5. Stop
I stopped after two consecutive failures (multicast, tensormap prefetch), about 2 h into the 3 h box.

**Untested next levers for the first-data term:**

- a red burst pipelined with the last stage.
