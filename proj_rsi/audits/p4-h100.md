# P4 on the H100 box: why pw24's wgmma rung cost +8 µs, and the fix (pw24h)

Box: H100 SXM, 1980 MHz. All numbers are in-prepare µs (graph replay after a 256 MB read flush, median of 31; the empty graph is ~3.5).
cuBLAS (`torch.mm(x, W.t())`) is timed in the same bake. Runs r1–r6 are `/root/okc/logs/pw24h-r*.log`.
- Shapes: pooler m=128 (N 4096, K 8192). A 64×64 tile gives 128 CTAs and 128 k64 stages.
- Code: `proj_rsi/candidates/pw24h/submission.py`, one templated kernel `bh<ST,BM,BN,CL,PP,WN,CG,RS,FN,PRB,INS,SY,KS>`.
- Correctness: every bake entry passes one-hot ×8 bit-exact and random rows at m and m−7. The harness CHECKs pass on all four layers at m = 128 and 256.

## 1. The mechanism

**(a) Most of the +8 µs belongs to the cluster.** The rung was measured on bg12c, the cluster-2 W-multicast variant.

| ladder (pooler m=128) | W-only | W+x | +wgmma | full |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| b12c, cluster (pw24's ladder) | 27.20 | 28.29 | 37.54 | 37.95 |
| b12, no cluster | 26.21 | 27.36 | 31.49–31.74 | 31.5–32.7 |

- The no-cluster wgmma rung is +4.2.
- The cluster adds about 6 more, but only when wgmma is present. The pair runs at the slower consumer's pace.

**(b) Smem-port contention is refuted.**

| probe | time | what it shows |
|---|---|---|
| L4: wgmma loop alone (no TMA) | 12.70 | 142 clk per stage, near the 128-clk tensor peak |
| L5: unthrottled W+x stream plus an *independent* wgmma loop on a stale slot | 27.07–27.39 | same as W+x: 32 KB of TMA writes plus wgmma reads per stage costs nothing at this rate |
| L6: normal ring flow, wgmma on a stale slot | 31.55 | same as L3: the cost comes from the dependency structure, not from which data the wgmma reads |

**(c) Holding the slot is not the cause; consumer service time is.**

| probe | time | delta vs W+x 27.26 |
|---|---|---|
| L7: no wgmma; release slot s−1 only after s lands (wait_group(1)'s hold) | 28.26 | +1.0 |
| L8: no wgmma; spin 240 clk per stage, then release | 33.82 | +6.5 |

- So whatever the single consumer warpgroup does serially per stage adds almost 1:1 to the period.
- From L8, the consumer's fixed per-stage cost (barrier waits, wake-up, release) is about 229 clk. That is 469 clk period − 240 spin, where 469 = (33.82 − 3.5) µs / 128 stages × 1980 clk/µs.
- The wgmma chain adds about 142 clk. Together that is a serial service time at or above the stream period (W+x: 371 clk per stage on the H100).

**clock64 instrumentation** (`.ins` variants, consumer warp 0, mean per k64, clk):

| variant | total | wait_in | fence+issue+commit | wait_group | release | producer empty-wait |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| b12 (pw24 no-cluster) | 474 | 167 | 106 | 39 | 91 | 298 |
| b12c (cluster) | 515 | 167 | 130 | 42 | 113 | 306 |
| fn1 (fence once) | 423 | 138 | 87 | 29 | 98 | 253 |
| w0 (wait_group 0, release at once) | 448 | 169 | 97 | 124 | 27 | 268 |
| mb (merged W+x barriers) | 383 | 108 | 91 | 29 | 85 | 201 |
| pp (ping-pong, per warpgroup = per 2 stages) | 701 | 260 | 189 | 67 | 93 | 127 |
| **k2s1** (BLOCK_K 128 + merged barriers) | **326** | 131 | 89 | 14 | 45 | 173 |
| rsk2 (same, register-A) | 322 | 145 | 113 | 9 | 30 | 180 |

**Mechanism.** pw24 has one consumer warpgroup. Per 64-k stage it does two `mbarrier.try_wait`s (each a suspend/wake), a fence, 4 HGMMA, a commit, a wait_group, a `__syncwarp` and 2–3 arrives, all serially.
- That bookkeeping (≈ 230 clk) plus the 128–142-clk wgmma chain is about as long as the stream period.
- The consumer therefore becomes the critical path, and producers stall on empty slots (empty-wait 298 vs 224 at W+x).
- The tensor work itself, smem bandwidth, and wait_group depth are not the limit.
- On the H200 the stream period is ~0.7× (≈ 260 clk per k64), so the same service time overshoots further. That explains why the judge showed +11 where the box shows +4.

## 3. Other findings

- **ptxas (7).**
  - pw24's structure compiles cleanly: no C7514, 58 regs, `__launch_bounds__(192,1)`, no setmaxnreg needed.
  - SASS per stage: 2 TRYWAIT, WARPGROUP.ARRIVE, 4 × (4 R2UR + HGMMA.64x64x16), DEPBAR.LE 1, 2–3 SYNCS.ARRIVE. There is no extra wait.
  - **Register-A trap:** `__float2bfloat16(D[i])` in the epilogue makes ptxas serialize the whole RS pipeline (C7514; 62 regs instead of 154). That is why "rs" was 50 µs.
  - Found by bisection (`pw24h/tools/rsbisect.py`): an inline-asm `cvt.rn.bf16x2.f32` removes it. **Any RS/decode kernel (RS128) must convert accumulators by inline asm.**
- **Groups in flight / commit granularity (1)(2).**
  - wait_group 1/2/3 and per-k16 commits change nothing.
  - What helps is *fewer sync rounds per byte*: fence once (−2.3), 2 stages per commit (−2.6), merged barriers (−3.2), BLOCK_K 128 (−4.0).
- **Ping-pong (3).** It hides most of the cost by overlapping two warpgroups' bookkeeping (pp L3 +1.5 over W+x), but loses to k2s1 at full kernel and is hurt by polling.
- **Cooperative 128×64 / 64×128 at m=128 (3).** These use 64 CTAs, which is too few: the W+x rung is fine but the full kernel is slow.
- **Register-A (4).** Once the sync is fixed it is equal to SS (rsk2 ≈ k2s1). It is *not* a smem-port relief at this rate, because L5 shows no port limit on the H100.
- **Release placement (5).** Releasing right after the stage's own wait_group 0 (w0) does not help. The hold is not the cost; consumer service time is.
- **Polling.** `mbarrier.test_wait` spinning instead of `try_wait` is worse whenever the consumer has other work, because the four spinning warps steal issue slots.
- **Cluster.** With k2s1 the W multicast costs +0.9 instead of +9. W-only is HBM-bound, so it still saves nothing.

## 4. What transfers to the H200 and what a judge candidate should carry

- **Mechanism.** It is consumer service time versus the stream period. The H200 period is about 0.7× the H100's, so the margin matters more there; the box understates the judge loss (bg12: +1.6 box, +4.7 judge).
- **k2s1 consumer budget.** About 150 clk of work per k64 (issue 89 + wait_group 14 + release 45) against a ~260-clk H200 period. The L8 spin of 120 clk per k64 costs only +0.7–0.9 on the box.
- **Carry:**
  - BLOCK_K = 128 slots (KS = 2), 7 × 32 KB, one consumer warpgroup.
  - One full barrier per slot (both producers `arrive.expect_tx` their own bytes, count 2) and one empty barrier (count 4: one arrive per consumer warp).
  - Suspending `try_wait`, not polling.
  - `wgmma.fence` once for SS; per slot for RS.
  - wait_group 1 and release s−1.
  - No cluster.
  - stmatrix.trans epilogue into a chunk-swizzled tile, with inline-asm bf16 conversion.
- **Tiles and picks:**
  - 64×64 at m = 128 for pooler and qkv (k2x / rsk2x).
  - 64×128 for pooler at m = 256, and for unpooler and o at m = 128 (n128x / n128x8).
  - Keep cuBLAS for qkv, unpooler and o at m = 256 until 128×128 two-warpgroup tiles exist.
- **For the 12-bit decode (RS128).** The decode adds about 50 issue clk per k64 to the same serial consumer. Budget it against the ~110 clk H200 slack of k2s1. If it does not fit, decode in a separate transform warpgroup or ping-pong two math warpgroups on alternate 128-k slots.
