# P3 on the H100: router m=128 / 256 (pw29 → pw29h), 2026-10-26

**Box:** root@107.170.6.60, H100 80GB HBM3, torch 2.8 cu128.
**Tools:**
- `proj_rsi/candidates/pw29h/rbench.py` is a router-only bench. It builds only the rw extension; pw23's gv extension comes from the cache. It validates every path with the submission's `_validate`, then times CUDA-graph replays of the whole call (memset node included).
- The flush is a 256 MB **read** (torch.sum), as the judge does (TASK.md: "reads a buffer larger than L2"). h100_bench's `flush.zero_()` leaves L2 dirty and changes the ranking at m=256 (see the end).
- Times are the median of per-round medians (5–7 rounds × 20 reps), in box-µs **including** the box's ~11–13 µs replay overhead. Compare only within a run.

## 0. pw29 as queued never ran its wgmma paths here
- pw29's box log says `PREP rw build failed`. Torch appends `-gencode compute_90/sm_90` unless TORCH_CUDA_ARCH_LIST is set, and ptxas rejects wgmma for sm_90.
- **Fixes in pw29h:**
  - `_rwext` sets `TORCH_CUDA_ARCH_LIST=9.0a` around the build and restores it after.
  - The rwk body and the wgmma helpers are guarded by `__CUDA_ARCH_FEAT_SM90_ALL`; a plain sm_90 pass compiles to a `__trap()` stub.
  - Verified: a dual-arch nvcc build of the generated source succeeds, with 0 spills.
- The coordinator says the judge's torch does not add sm_90. The guard makes the build robust either way.

## 2. Where the time goes (ladder, kernel-side deltas, read flush)
**m=128, rw64k32s8:**
- loads only, no reds: 2.16
- \+ wgmma: +0.67
- \+ reds (4 MB v4): +1.2
- the memset node: ≈ +1.4. The memset alone is ~0.1; the cost is the graph's memset→kernel edge.
- Full kernel without the memset: 2.54; with self-zeroing: 3.10.

**m=256, rw64k32r3x2:**
- loads only, no reds: 2.13
- \+ wgmma: +1.8. hi+lo doubles the tensor work to 2.15 GFLOP, about 2.2 µs at the chip's peak, so this is inherent.
- \+ reds (8 MB): +2.8
- no memset: 4.94; self-zero: 5.57.

**Self-zeroing:**
- v1 (first arriver zeroes the whole tile, flag): lost (+0.2 / +1.1). Its atomic return sits behind 128 KB of cp.async data, putting the zeroing on the tail.
- v2 (each K-split CTA zeroes 1/G of the tile after issuing its loads, `atom.add.release`, poll at the end): −0.65.
- v3 (the poll moved before the last `wgmma.wait_group`): another −0.4. Final overhead is ~0.55 over the ideal no-zero kernel.

## 4. What transfers to the H200
- **Memset-node removal transfers fully.** The judge's pw23 profile already showed the 0.9 µs node. Graph-edge latency does not scale with HBM.
- **wgmma vs mma.sync transfers.** It is a tensor-issue and latency structure, the same on both cards.
- **Not expected to transfer:** the box's stream-bound load phase (~2.2 µs) scales ~0.7×.
- **Expected judge gain at m=128:** −1.0 to −1.5 µs vs pw23's rp64k32s4 (≈ 8–12 score-µs at 7.9 per µs).
- **m=256:** the reds (+2.8) and tensor (+1.8) phases do not shrink with HBM; cuBLAS shares the tensor cost. Expect parity ±0.3. The bake decides with MARGIN 0.99.
- **Closed on this box:**
  - cluster DSMEM pre-reduction at 2, 4 and 8;
  - red-order rotation;
  - KR-512 rings (pathological, 24+ µs; cause not chased);
  - 256 tokens per CTA (the TOKS-256 wgmma tensor phase is exposed);
  - 2-wave grids at m=256.
- **Untested levers:**
  - TMA tensor loads with multicast of x across the 4 n-tiles;
  - a working KR-512 / 16-split path to halve the m=256 reds.

## 5. Risks of the self-zeroing paths
- **Contiguous arrivals.** The G arrivals of one call to a tile must be contiguous: calls stream-ordered, and never two router calls of the same variant running concurrently on two streams. Graph replays and the judge satisfy this.
- **One co-resident wave.** Every shipped self-zeroing grid is one co-resident wave: 128 CTAs at 1/SM, or 256 CTAs at 2/SM with occupancy 2 → 264 slots. Even then, waiting CTAs only wait for tile mates that are running or about to be scheduled.
- **Counter wrap.** `st["rw_cnt"]` (8192 int32) is monotonic and never reset; it wraps after ~67M calls of one variant.
